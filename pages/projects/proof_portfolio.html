Example Proof

Problem 33.

Notice that

$$\begin{align*} 1&=1 \\ 1+3&=4 \\ 1+3+5&=9 \\ 1+3+5+7&=16 \\ 1+3+5+7+9&=25. \end{align*}$$

Make a conjecture about the sum of odd numbers by giving a general formula for what you see above. Then prove that your formula is valid by using mathematical induction.

Solution.

We will prove that, for all natural numbers \(x\), we know the statement \((2(1)-1)+(2(2)-1)+...+(2x-1)=x^2\) is true. If we simplify the expression \((2(1)-1)+(2(2)-1)+...+(2x-1)\) we find that it is equal to \(1+3+5+...+(2x-1)\). Let \(S=\{x \in \mathbb{N}\mid 1+3+5+...+(2x-1)=x^2\}\). We will show that \(S\) is the set of all natural numbers, or \(S= \mathbb{N}\). We will use the Principle of Mathematical Induction. We know that \(2(1)-1 = 1 = 1^2\), therefore \(1 \in S\). Let \(k \in \mathbb{N}\). Suppose \(k \in S\). We know \(1+3+5+...+(2k-1)=k^2\). We want to show \(1+3+5+...+(2k-1)+(2(k+1)-1)=(k+1)^2\). We compute:

$$\begin{align*} \underbrace{1+2+3+\cdots + (2k-1)} + (2(k+1)-1) &=k^2 + (2(k+1)-1) && \text{ (replace using the assumption) }\\ &=k^2 + 2k+2-1 && \text{ (simplify) }\\ &= k^2 + 2k+1 && \text{ (simplify) }\\ &=(k+1)^2 && \text{(factor).} \end{align*}$$

Thus we see that \(1+3+5+...+(2k-1)+(2(k+1)-1)=(k+1)^2\). Therefore, we know \(k+1 \in S\). Because we know \(k+1 \in S\), we see that if an element \(k \in S\) then \(k+1 \in S\). Since we know \(1\in S\) and if an element \(k \in S\) then \(k+1 \in S\) we know by the Principle of Mathematical Induction that \(S= \mathbb{N}\).
